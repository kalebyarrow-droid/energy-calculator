How electricity is used

Battery only

Charge from the grid between midnight and 6am at the overnight rate. Reserve stored energy for the expensive 4–9pm peak first, then use any remaining energy during other higher-priced hours. Charging and discharging losses are included.

Solar + battery

Solar powers daytime household loads first. Excess solar charges the battery; remaining excess is exported. Battery energy is used during the evening, prioritising peak hours. If there is spare battery capacity, cheap overnight grid charging can supplement solar charging.

EV charging

Occasional EV charging is assigned to the midnight–6am grid tariff, not supplied from the house battery. Enter charging frequency and energy per session above. If your saved bills already include EV charging, leave “already included” at 1 to avoid double counting.

Model limitation: Bill totals do not reveal the exact time of consumption. Battery dispatch is an approximate daily energy allocation, not a validated hour-by-hour schedule. EV charging and battery charging may be constrained by your charger and household supply limits.

What the bill predictions are based on

Electricity consumption

Saved bills are combined into a billing-day-weighted daily average for each tariff band. That same daily usage pattern is repeated across all 12 months. Seasonal household usage changes are not currently forecast. If there are no saved bills, the current bill-entry values are used instead.

Solar generation

Solar output does vary by season: a monthly generation factor is applied to the daily solar yield setting, with stronger summer output and weaker winter output. These are generic estimates, not a roof-specific weather forecast.

Battery, EV and future years

Battery savings use the averaged tariff-band consumption, battery capacity, charging efficiency and assumed dispatch. EV charging is allocated to the overnight tariff. Future years apply your chosen electricity price increase, solar/battery degradation, maintenance and investment return; they do not forecast changing household behaviour or EV charging frequency.

How to interpret: This is a scenario model, not a prediction from seasonal consumption or half-hourly meter readings. For better accuracy, enter a full 12 months of bills; interval meter data would be needed to model seasonal loads and charging times properly.
